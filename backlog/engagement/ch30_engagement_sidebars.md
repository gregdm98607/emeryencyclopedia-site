## SIDEBAR 1: Trivia Callout — "The Last Unmapped Swell"

**Title**: "Did You Know? The Last Unmapped Swell"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.1 intro paragraph (before `---` section break)

**Text**:
> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> The San Rafael Swell was so remote and rugged that it was one of the last major geographic features in the contiguous United States to be systematically mapped. John Wesley Powell's 1869 and 1871–72 expeditions documented the Green River corridor, but the interior of the Swell remained poorly charted for decades after. The same canyons that defeated systematic survey in the nineteenth century still swallow cell signals today.

**Purpose**: Anchors the section's claim about the Swell's remoteness with a concrete historical fact; transitions naturally into the practical access guidance that follows.

## SIDEBAR 2: Field Itinerary — "The Buckhorn Draw Day Loop"

**Title**: "Field Guide: The Buckhorn Draw Day Loop"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.2 Wedge Overlook subsection (replacing the inline `> FAMILY ACTIVITY` blockquote and the "Field Trip" in the former Engagement Features section)

**Text**:
> **FIELD GUIDE: The Buckhorn Draw Day Loop**
> **Getting there**: From Castle Dale, drive south on Emery County Road 332 (Buckhorn Draw Road). Paved briefly, then graded dirt — high-clearance recommended; call BLM Price Field Office (435-636-3600) to check conditions after rain. **Time needed**: Full day (5–6 hours with stops, ~80 miles round trip). **Best season**: Spring and fall. **Bring**: 1 gallon water/person, binoculars, lunch, sun hat.
> **On the ground**: Stop at the Buckhorn Wash Pictograph Panel (3.5 miles past the swinging bridge) for the Barrier Canyon figures — then walk across the 1937 CCC swinging bridge over the San Rafael River. Continue to the Wedge Overlook for lunch at the canyon rim; 1,200 feet below, the river bends through Utah's Little Grand Canyon. Canyon walls are prime raptor habitat — scan with binoculars in the morning. Return via the same road or exit south to I-70 for a loop route back to Castle Dale.
> **See also**: Chapter 12 (Rock Art), Chapter 2 (Deep-Time Geology)

**Purpose**: Converts the most actionable content in the chapter into a usable day-trip plan; captures both the §30.2 inline activity and the "Field Trip" from the Engagement Features section in a single, richer element.

> **SCAVENGER HUNT: Slot Canyon Science** — *Can you find all three inside Little Wild Horse Canyon?*
> 1. A place where you can touch both canyon walls at the same time — the narrowest passage in the narrows
> 2. A pothole: a round depression worn into the canyon floor by swirling water and sand over centuries
> 3. A flute: a curved groove carved into the canyon wall where flood water cut a channel
> **Bonus challenge**: Find a chockstone — a boulder wedged between the canyon walls by a past flood — and photograph it.

**Text**:
> **TRY THIS: Goblin Valley Explorer Challenge**
> **What you need**: Camera, meeting time, curiosity | **Ages**: 5+ | **Time**: 45–90 min
> Give each person a different goblin shape to find and photograph — a mushroom, a toadstool, an elephant, a face in the rock. The valley floor has no trails; everyone scatters freely. **Title**: "Did You Know? Galaxy Quest's Real Alien Planet"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.4 Geology Note (after the FamilyActivity, before `---` break)

**Text**:
> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> Goblin Valley State Park appeared in *Galaxy Quest* (1999), where it served as the surface of an alien planet — a fitting role for a landscape so genuinely strange that visitors regularly describe it as looking like it belongs on another world. The goblins are eroded from Middle Jurassic Entrada Sandstone deposited roughly 170 million years ago, long before any life on Earth would have recognized a coastline, let alone a film crew.

**Purpose**: The movie connection is the chapter's most recognizable pop-culture hook; pairing it immediately with the age of the formation makes the geology feel visceral rather than academic.

## SIDEBAR 6: Trivia Callout — "World-Class, Both Ends of the Scale"

**Title**: "Did You Know? World-Class at Both Ends"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.5 Practical Notes

**Text**:
> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> Joe's Valley bouldering problems are graded from V0 — manageable for a motivated first-timer — to V14, among the hardest grades recorded anywhere in the sport. Elite climbers fly from Europe, Japan, and Australia to attempt the hardest problems; families with no climbing experience routinely find routes they can top in an afternoon. Very few places in the world can honestly claim both.

## SIDEBAR 7: Factbox — "Green River: Two Trips, Two Experiences"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.7 Green River section (before `### Joe's Valley Reservoir`)

> **GREEN RIVER: TWO TRIPS, TWO EXPERIENCES**
> | | Labyrinth Canyon | Desolation/Gray Canyons |
> |---|---|---|
> | Distance | 68 miles | 84 miles |
> | Difficulty | Flat water — canoe/kayak/raft | Class III–IV rapids — experienced paddlers only |

## SIDEBAR 8: Trivia Callout — "A Bridge Built for Cattle"

**Title**: "Did You Know? A Bridge Built for Cattle"

**Location in Chapter**: After §30.8 Buckhorn Draw Scenic Backway paragraph

**Text**:
> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> The Buckhorn Draw Swinging Bridge was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1937 to allow cattle crossings of the San Rafael River — at the time, the only bridge over the river for miles in either direction. Cattle were driven across it seasonally for decades. It is now a historical landmark that hikers walk across for the experience of crossing a suspension bridge over a canyon, 85 years after the last cattle drive used it for the same purpose.

**Purpose**: The CCC connection makes the bridge more than a curiosity stop; the 85-year continuity of use is a quietly surprising human-scale detail.

## SIDEBAR 9: Photo Assignment — "Three Zones, One County"

**Location in Chapter**: After Sources section (end of file)

**Text**:
> **PHOTO CHALLENGE: Three Zones, One County**
> **Your subject**: One image per terrain zone — all three on a single trip.
> 1. The Wasatch Plateau — high green country above 7,000 ft, with forest and reservoir
> 2. The San Rafael Swell — red rock, canyon walls, or open desert
> 3. The Green River corridor — the river itself, or the canyon walls above it
> **What to look for**: Try to capture each image in the same quality of light (golden hour works beautifully across all three zones). Notice how completely different each environment looks despite all being within a single county.
> **The story you're telling**: Same county. Three entirely different worlds. That vertical and horizontal range — from high-mountain spruce forest to desert slot canyon to river wilderness — is Emery County's defining geographic fact.
> *Share with #EmeryEncyclopedia*

**Purpose**: The photo challenge motivates a multi-zone trip and asks readers to hold the whole county in mind simultaneously — the central claim of the chapter's opening paragraph.
